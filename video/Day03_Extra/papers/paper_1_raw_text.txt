Abstract
In recent years, the automatic recognition of invoices has emerged as a critical 
component in document digitization, particularly within the financial sector, where 
efficient data extraction from structured documents is essential. This paper introduces 
an advanced method for automating invoice recognition by combining cutting-
edge image preprocessing, OCR pipeline techniques, and post-processing strategies. 
The process begins with a robust image preprocessing stage that includes scaling, 
binarization, noise reduction, and skew correction based on Hough transform; 
learning-based angle regressors may offer adaptive alternatives for noisy or multi-
aligned invoices, all of which enhance the quality of the input images, ensuring 
optimal performance during OCR. The core of the system is an OCR pipeline that 
integrates state-of-the-art text detection and recognition algorithms, effectively 
handling various challenges such as multi-oriented and densely packed text 
regions. After the initial recognition, a series of post-processing steps, including error 
correction, field validation, and structured data formatting, are applied to ensure that 
the extracted text is accurate and conforms to predefined invoice formats. Extensive 
experiments conducted on a dataset comprising 6349 images, including both 
electronic and paper invoices, demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves 
high recognition accuracy and reliable performance. These results highlight the 
system’s effectiveness in automating invoice recognition, confirming its applicability 
in real-world financial applications where precision and efficiency are paramount. The 
method provides a comprehensive solution for improving invoice digitization and 
streamlining financial workflows.
Keywords  Optical character recognition, Text detection algorithms, Text recognition

exploring the adoption of advanced information technologies to enhance work efficiency 
and management standards [1]. In the electric power industry, particularly in the area 
of bill management for power grid enterprises, traditional manual identification meth­
ods are becoming increasingly inadequate due to their inefficiency and susceptibility to 
errors. As a key foundation for power trading [2], settlement, and financial management,

In recent years, Optical Character Recognition (OCR) technology has gained wide­
spread use across various sectors, including finance, insurance, healthcare, and trans­
portation. OCR technology [3] significantly streamlines the process of text data entry 
by converting text from paper documents or images into editable formats. However,

Studies have shown that the accuracy and efficiency of text recognition can be greatly 
enhanced by leveraging pre-trained OCR large models [5]. Research suggests that deep 
learning-based OCR techniques hold great potential in text recognition [6], particularly 
when applied to large and diverse datasets. However, most of these studies focus on gen­
eral-purpose text recognition, with relatively few addressing the specific needs of power 
grid enterprises.
In recent years, advancements in Optical Character Recognition (OCR) technologies 
have significantly improved the accuracy and efficiency of text detection and recognition 
in complex document formats such as electronic invoices [7]. Traditional OCR systems 
often struggled with challenges posed by variable text orientations, dense layouts, and 
irregular text shapes. However, modern algorithms such as EAST (Efficient and Accu­
rate Scene Text Detector), DB (Differentiable Binarization), and SAST (Segmentation-
based Scene Text Detector) have revolutionized the text detection phase by offering 
robust solutions for handling multi-oriented, curved, and densely packed text regions. 
These detection methods, when combined with powerful recognition models like CRNN 
(Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network), which captures sequential text information, 
and RARE (Robust Attention-based Recognition), which excels in recognizing distorted 
or irregular text, enable highly accurate and efficient OCR pipelines. By integrating these 
algorithms, the recognition of complex document structures—such as invoices—has 
become more reliable, ensuring that extracted data is both precise and well-structured 
for downstream applications. This progress has laid the foundation for more effective 
document digitization and automation in financial systems, driving improvements in 
areas such as invoice processing and data extraction.
Against this backdrop, this study seeks to address the inefficiencies and error-prone 
nature of manual bill recognition in power grid enterprises by introducing an intelligent 
bill recognition and verification method based on a pre-trained OCR macromodel. This 
study [8] follows a systematic approach: first, by collecting and annotating large-scale 
image-text datasets; second, by conducting model training and optimization; and finally, 
by validating the model’s recognition performance through experimental testing.
This research not only provides technical support for the refined management of 
smart grids but also promotes the automation and standardization of bill manage­
ment processes [9]. Specifically, by enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of bill rec­
ognition, this study significantly improves business processing efficiency, reduces labor 
costs, and minimizes human error, thereby providing strong support for the refined 
management of smart grids. Furthermore, this study is expected to encourage broader

2.2  OCR pipeline
In the OCR pipeline for electronic invoice recognition, two primary stages are involved: 
text detection and text recognition. The text detection phase employs several advanced 
algorithms, including EAST, DB, and SAST, to accurately identify the regions in the 
image that contain text. EAST (Efficient and Accurate Scene Text) [10] is a robust and 
fast algorithm used for detecting text regions in arbitrary orientations, while DB (Dif­
ferentiable Binarization) [11] and SAST (Segmentation-based Scene Text) [12] further 
refine the detection process, offering enhanced precision, particularly in dense or curved 
text regions. Specifically, EAST is a robust and fast algorithm designed for detecting 
text regions in arbitrary orientations. Unlike traditional methods that involve multiple 
processing steps, EAST directly predicts text regions and bounding boxes using a fully 
convolutional neural network (FCN). This simplicity results in faster inference times 
while maintaining high accuracy. EAST is an anchor-free detector, particularly suited for 
scenarios with irregular text layouts, making it ideal for detecting text in complex doc­
uments such as electronic invoices. EAST algorithm uses a multi-task loss, where geom­
etry and score losses are normalized to prevent imbalance during training that combines 
geometry and segmentation losses, typically expressed as:

3  Experimental results
3.1  Datasets description
To train a high-performance OCR model, it is essential to prepare large-scale and 
diverse image-text datasets. Thousands of paper and electronic invoices were collected 
from NEC to construct the dataset. OCR technology was utilized to extract text from 
the images, leveraging either open-source OCR libraries or cloud-based OCR services. 
Once the text was extracted, the image and corresponding text were annotated. The 
annotation process involved eit her directly linking the extracted text with the image or 
highlighting the text area within the image by drawing bounding boxes around the text 


=== PAGE 8 ===
Page 8 of 12
Wang et al. Discover Artificial Intelligence           (2026) 6:636 
and associating the corresponding content. This approach allows the model to learn the 
relationships between image regions and the text they contain.
Following the data labeling, the dataset underwent preprocessing to enhance the 
model’s performance during training. The data was cleaned to eliminate inaccurately or 
incompletely labeled samples. Automated tools were employed to facilitate this process, 
including the removal of duplicate data and correction of erroneous annotations. These 
steps ensure that the dataset is high-quality and optimized for training the OCR model.
In this study, a total of 6349 images were used as the dataset, consisting of 4618 elec­
tronic invoices and the remaining images representing paper invoices. The dataset was 
divided into a training set, validation set, and test set, following the commonly used ratio 
of 7:2:1. This division ensures that the model is trained on a substantial portion of the 
data, while also allowing for validation and testing to evaluate its performance and gen­
eralization ability on unseen data.
3.2  The performance of text detection
We conducted experiments to evaluate the performance of text detection in the OCR 
pipeline after image preprocessing, as shown in Fig. 2. The image on the top represents 
an electronic invoice, while the bottom side illustrates the OCR text detection results, 
where the text regions are highlighted and segmented. The OCR system successfully 
identifies and extracts several key fields from the invoice, including the invoice number, 
date, and various financial details. The text areas are precisely boxed, showcasing the 
system’s capability to detect text in both horizontal and vertical orientations. Most of 
the text fields, such as invoice codes and monetary amounts, are captured accurately, 
although minor misalignments are observed in densely populated areas. Overall, the 
OCR detection is effective in extracting a comprehensive set of invoice details, which 
can subsequently be used in further stages of the pipeline, such as text recognition and 
data validation.

3.3  The performance of text recognition

4  Discussion and conclusions
In this study, we explored the application of OCR-based intelligent ticket recognition 
and verification technology in the context of power grid ticket management. Our system 
is built upon a robust OCR pipeline that integrates advanced algorithms such as EAST 
for text detection and CRNN for text recognition, ensuring high precision in detecting 
and recognizing key fields within invoices. Through comprehensive experiments and 
case studies, the large OCR model demonstrated outstanding performance, achieving 
a recognition accuracy of 0.96 for electronic invoices and over 0.97 for key fields such 
as invoice numbers and dates. These results affirm the efficiency and accuracy of OCR

Our proposed system is structured around a well-defined encoder-decoder architec­

ally, although this study focuses on the technical capabilities of OCR, important issues 
such as security and compliance—critical in grid ticket management—have been rela­
tively underexplored.
In future work, efforts should focus on improving recognition accuracy in challenging 
environmental conditions by refining algorithms such as SAST, which could offer better 
segmentation of irregularly shaped text. Expanding the dataset to include a broader vari­
ety of bill types will also be essential for making the system more generalizable. Further­
more, addressing security and privacy concerns will be crucial to ensuring compliance 
with industry regulations. OCR technology has the potential to extend its application 
beyond power grid management into sectors like finance, insurance, healthcare, and 
education, paving the way for a more extensive digital transformation across industries.

multi-scale feature extraction. Exploring synthetic data augmentation using diffusion
